These are simplified monthly numbers so the trade-offs are easy to see. The point is to notice patterns and pressure points, not to pretend these are real accounts.

Monthly income—

What comes in each month, before anything goes out. There's no universal "good" number here — what matters is how it compares to what goes out below.

Monthly expenses—

Everything that goes out each month. A commonly cited general guideline (the "50/30/20" rule) is roughly 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings/debt repayment — a reference point, not a rule to force.

Savings buffer—

Cash set aside for emergencies. A commonly cited benchmark is 3–6 months of essential expenses — more if your income is unpredictable.

Investments—

Money working toward longer-term goals. One widely-cited general benchmark suggests roughly 1x your annual income invested by 30, growing from there — a reference point, not a personalized target.

Debt balance—

Total owed across debts. What matters most is this relative to income — see the debt-to-income ratio below. Many guides cite keeping total debt payments under roughly 36% of income as generally more manageable.

Illustrative net worth-

Savings and investments, minus debt. There's no fixed "good" number — it varies hugely by age and circumstances. The trend over time matters more than any single figure.

What these numbers mean

Emergency fund ratio — how many months your savings would cover essential expenses. A healthy buffer usually sits around 3–6 months.

Debt-to-income ratio — how much of your income is going toward debt payments. Lower is usually easier to manage.

How to read this panel — the main thing to watch is whether your choices are making you more buffered, more fragile, or more overextended.
